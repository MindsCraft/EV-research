01

Market State of Play — The Big Picture

As of June 2026, the Bangladesh EV charging market is in its very earliest commercial stages — but moving faster than commonly reported. Official BERC data confirmed just 3 days ago (June 27, 2026) shows 32 charging stations now approved, with 9 already built and operational — a meaningful jump from the 14-station figure that circulated throughout 2025. The government target remains 1,200 stations. The gap is still enormous, and multiple players are racing to fill it before the competitive window closes.

Key Finding: CrackPlatoon MD Tenver Sahariar Utso confirmed to The Daily Star (May 2026) that "in April 2026, we used around 4 megawatts of electricity to commercially charge nearly 330 vehicles at our stations." This is real, live revenue — not just infrastructure. The market has started generating actual charging sessions.

Newest Finding (3 days old): At a DCCI/BSREA seminar on June 27, 2026, BERC Member (Electricity) Abul Khair Md Aminur Rahman confirmed 32 stations approved, 9 built. Industries Secretary Abdun Naser Khan confirmed a draft national EV policy is ready post-consultation. BEPRC Chairman proposed a dedicated inter-agency coordination body — meaning multiple ministries currently handle EV policy with no single point of contact. This regulatory fragmentation is both a risk (slower approvals) and an opportunity (early movers who build relationships across agencies gain disproportionate advantage).

Watch Point: Even with 32 BERC-approved stations, only 9 are physically built — a 72% execution gap. This confirms that regulatory approval is not the bottleneck; physical deployment speed is. Whoever moves fastest from approval to construction wins the location race. This favours operators (like you) who pre-negotiate supplier delivery timelines before seeking approval, rather than after.

🏛️ Policy Window — Move Before the Coordination Body Forms

As of June 27, 2026, the government still lacks a single coordinating body for EV policy — multiple ministries (Power Division, Industries, BERC, SREDA, BRTA) each handle a piece. A draft national EV policy is ready but not finalised, and BEPRC has formally proposed an inter-agency coordination body. Once that body forms, approval processes will likely tighten and formalise — good for long-term legitimacy, but it could also mean slower, more bureaucratic approvals for new entrants. Your IRC and SREDA applications should be filed in the current fragmented-but-faster window, not after consolidation.
